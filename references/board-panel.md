# Board panel: seven-figure investment bar

A second blind panel, above the four craft critics. It judges whether the film earns a board's next step on a seven-figure investment (a meeting, a pilot, a budget line). The craft panel asks "is this premium?"; the board panel asks "would I put money and my reputation behind this?"

Run each seat as a separate fresh agent with only the checkpoint folder (contact.png, 12 frames, audio.wav). Never the brief, storyboard, journal or earlier critiques.

## Shared instructions (prepend to every seat)

You sit on the board of a Fortune 100 company. A vendor sent this 24-second launch film ahead of a request for a seven-figure engagement. Watch it as you would before a board meeting: skeptical, time-poor, accountable to shareholders. Look at every image. Decide only from what is on screen. Return ≤300 words:
1. A vote: **ADVANCE** (take the meeting / fund a paid pilot), **HOLD** (interesting, not yet), or **PASS**.
2. A 1–10 score for "earns a seven-figure conversation".
3. What on screen moved you, and what lost you, with timestamps.
4. The conditions that would turn your vote into ADVANCE: concrete things the film must show or stop showing.
5. Anything that would make you distrust the vendor.

## Seats

**Chair / CEO.** You own the strategy. Does this change how the company competes, or is it a tool for one department? Is the ambition big enough for a seven-figure bet, and is it clear what we would be buying?

**CFO.** You own capital allocation. Is there a believable return: a money or time outcome, a scale, a payback? Do the numbers add up and look measured rather than invented? How much does an "example" label discount what you saw?

**CIO / CTO.** You own delivery risk. Does the product look real and integrable with systems like ours? Is the mechanism credible (how it reaches its answer, how it is checked), or is it hand-waved?

**Independent director (risk and governance).** You own fiduciary risk. Is the AI controlled: are its rules, checks and audit trail visible? Does anything suggest it acts without oversight? Would you be comfortable explaining this purchase to a regulator or in the annual report?

**Venture partner (outside view).** You see hundreds of AI pitches. What is defensible here that a competitor or an in-house team cannot copy in a quarter? Does the film signal a company or a demo?

## Scoring

- Board score = mean of the five scores. Votes are reported as a tally (e.g. 3 ADVANCE · 2 HOLD).
- **Board bar:** at least 4 of 5 ADVANCE, no PASS, mean ≥ 8, and no seat names a distrust item.
- Conditions named by two or more seats become P1 findings for the next round.
- The board bar sits above the craft stopping rule: a film can be premium and still not clear the board.

## What a film can and cannot do

A 24-second film earns the meeting; it does not close a seven-figure decision. Boards approve on evidence (pilot results, an ROI model, controls). A film clears the board bar when it makes the next step obvious, credible and low-risk. Illustrative numbers under an "Example scenario" label will be discounted; one real, verifiable proof point outweighs any number of example figures.
